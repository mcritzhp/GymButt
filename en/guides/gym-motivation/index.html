Sticking with the gym

Motivation is a bad training plan. These eight habits work even on tired days.

In short

People who stick with it don't rely on motivation but on fixed training days, a small minimum goal and planned rest days. A missed workout isn't a problem as long as the next one happens. Friends and visible progress make it easier.

1. Fixed days instead of "when I have time"

Pick two or three days and put them in your calendar like an appointment. "Monday, Wednesday, Friday after work" is easier to keep than "three times a week".

2. A minimum goal that always works

On bad days the rule is: 20 minutes or three exercises. Once you're changed and at the gym, you usually do more. And if not, you still hit your minimum.

3. Plan your rest days

A plan without breaks falls apart at the first cold. Planned rest days are part of training and don't feel like failure.

4. Never miss twice in a row

Missing a workout happens. In a study on habit formation (Lally et al., 2010), a single missed day didn't disrupt the habit. What matters is the next session.

5. Decide the workout in advance

If you only figure out at the gym what to do today, you lose time and energy. A fixed plan with exercises and weights makes the decision for you.

6. Make progress visible

Muscles grow slowly, numbers don't. Seeing your deadlift go from 40 to 60 kg brings you back next week.

7. Train with friends

People cancel plans with others less often than plans with themselves. It doesn't have to be the same gym: it's enough to see that the others have already trained today.

8. Small rewards

A check mark in the calendar, a streak or a new item for your avatar: small, immediate rewards make the start easier until training runs on its own.

FAQ

What should I do when I have no motivation for the gym?

Don't rely on motivation. Rely on fixed training days and a small minimum goal, for example just 20 minutes. Most of the time you end up training longer. Planned rest days, a training partner and visible progress help too.

How long does it take for exercise to become a habit?

In a study by Lally et al. (2010) it took 66 days on average for a new behavior to become automatic, with a wide range from 18 to 254 days. Missing a single day didn't break the habit.

Is it bad to miss a workout?

No. A single missed day hardly matters. What counts is training again at the next planned session. Just don't miss two workouts in a row.

Source: Lally P et al., European Journal of Social Psychology 2010, "How are habits formed: Modelling habit formation in the real world".
